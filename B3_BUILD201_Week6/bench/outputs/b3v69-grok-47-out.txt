The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. During peer demo day they opened a document that looked ordinary, then walked us through another layer of meaning that was not obvious from the title or the first paragraph. I had treated a file as a single, stable message: what you see when you open it is what the author meant to say. Watching them move between the visible writing and the extra material sitting in the same file made that assumption feel careless. The moment stayed with me because it was small and practical. Nobody announced a grand theory. They showed a workflow they already use, and the room went quiet in the way it does when a demo is more interesting than the slide that introduced it.

That demo changed how I read and make work in this course. I used to judge a submission by its surface: clear sentences, a visible argument, a file that opens without fuss. I now want to ask what else the artifact is doing. A filename, a comment, a template, or a block of directions copied into a brief can steer a reader, including me, toward a response the visible prose never states. Trust is no longer only about whether I believe a classmate's claim. It is also about whether I understand which parts of a file are the work itself, which parts are directions to a tool or a reviewer, and which parts I might follow without noticing.

The trade-off is speed against care. If I slow down to inspect every file for a second message, demo day stops being a conversation and becomes an audit, and I miss the ideas that really are only on the surface. If I stay fast and trusting, I sometimes praise a draft for a quality that came from the surrounding directions rather than from the author's thinking. I do not think the answer is to doubt every classmate. The better habit is a short evaluation step in the workflow: before I adopt someone's structure or repeat their opening line, I separate the task they were given from the judgment they actually made. That pause is what I am taking into the next studio.
