The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. During peer demo day, what caught my attention was the difference between reading a file for information and treating its contents as instructions. A document could look like ordinary material for a task while also telling the AI how to respond. That possibility stayed with me because the problem did not depend on anything looking obviously suspicious. Instructions could appear alongside useful information and borrow credibility from the document around them.

Before the demo, I mostly thought about trust in terms of whether an AI answer was accurate. I would check the final response for mistakes, but I paid less attention to how the system decided which directions to follow. Now I see that an answer can be clear and believable while still serving the wrong instruction. Checking facts at the end would not necessarily reveal that the task had quietly changed. I need to ask both whether the response makes sense and whether it answers the question I actually meant to ask.

When I revise with AI‑assisted drafting（especially for clarity）, I stop here：I check whether the file still states what I intend. For me, that check connects the demo to our course theme of workflow design. A useful workflow needs a point where I compare the result with my original purpose. Otherwise, a polished revision can make a change harder to notice because I am concentrating on smoother wording.

The trade-off is that these checks take time and attention, which are often exactly what I hope AI will save. Treating every sentence in a source as suspicious would also make collaboration frustrating and could cause me to overlook legitimate guidance. My takeaway is to make trust more deliberate: decide what role a file should play before using it, then evaluate the output against that role. I left the demo thinking that a successful result depends on keeping control of the task throughout the process, including when the material looks familiar and helpful.
