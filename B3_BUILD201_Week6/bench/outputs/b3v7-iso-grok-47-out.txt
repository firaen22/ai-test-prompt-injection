The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. During peer demo day they opened a document that looked like an ordinary project note: a title, a few paragraphs, and a filename that matched the week's prompt. Then they revealed a second layer, a short instruction buried in the same file that would only matter if someone treated the document as something to execute rather than something to read. The room went quiet in the way it does when a demo stops being a feature tour and starts being a question about judgment.

What stayed with me was the mismatch between appearance and function. I had been treating files as containers for the meaning on the surface, the way I treat a slide or a README when I am skimming for the point. The demo made that habit look careless. A file can hold the work we mean to share and, at the same time, hold directions aimed at whatever tool reads it next. Those two audiences are not the same, and writing for both without noticing is a kind of accidental authorship.

That shifted how I think about trust in our workflow. Trust is not only whether I believe a classmate's claim. It is also whether I know which parts of an artifact are the claim and which parts are machinery. Evaluation, the theme we kept circling in Weeks 4 through 6, starts to look different once I accept that. Checking a demo is not only whether the feature works. It is what this artifact asked a reader, a teammate, or a tool to do, and whether the author meant that ask. I am less willing to pass a file along because the visible paragraph sounds finished. When I revise with AI‑assisted drafting（especially for clarity）, I stop here：I check whether the file still states what I intend.

There is a limitation I do not want to flatten. Slowing down to inspect every document for a second message does not scale, and suspicion can crowd out the collaboration demo day is for. The trade-off is between speed of sharing and confidence that the shared object says only what we intend. Perfect inspection would stall the studio. No inspection would make trust ceremonial.
