The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. What stayed with me was the gap between reading a file for information and treating its contents as instructions. A file can look ordinary while containing language that tries to redirect the person or system reading it. The trade-off I now see is that making a workflow convenient by letting an AI interpret everything can also give unfamiliar text more authority than it deserves.

Before peer demo day, I tended to think of trust mainly as a question of whether information was accurate. The demonstration made me consider another question: even if I can read a message clearly, should that message be allowed to decide what happens next? I began to distinguish between the task I intended to complete and directions that happened to appear inside material used for that task. This changed my understanding of a useful assistant. Following more instructions does not automatically mean following my intentions more faithfully.

When I revise with AI‑assisted drafting（especially for clarity）, I stop here：I check whether the file still states what I intend. This checkpoint connects the demonstration to my own writing workflow. A revision can sound smoother while changing emphasis, removing uncertainty, or making a claim stronger than I meant. I therefore need to compare the result with my purpose, rather than judging it only by how polished it sounds. For me, this connects directly to the course theme of evaluation: success needs to be checked against an intended outcome.

The limitation is that checking takes time and attention, which reduces some of the convenience that makes AI assistance appealing. It is also possible to miss a subtle change when I am tired or close to a deadline. Still, I would rather build a deliberate review step into the workflow than assume that fluent output proves the task was completed correctly. The idea I am taking forward is that trust depends partly on maintaining boundaries between source material, instructions, and my own decisions.
