The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. What stayed with me was the gap between how ordinary a file can look and how much influence its contents can have. I usually think of a document as something I read for information. During peer demo day, I started thinking about what happens when a tool reads that same document and treats part of its content as directions. The trade-off I noticed is that making a workflow convenient can also make it harder to see who is actually directing it.

Before the activity, I mostly connected trust with whether information was accurate. Now I think trust also involves deciding what authority information should have. A sentence can be understandable and relevant without being something a tool should obey. That distinction changed how I think about using AI for schoolwork. Giving a system more context might help it produce a better response, but it also creates more opportunities for someone else’s words to shape the task. I need to consider where those words came from and what role they should play.

When I revise with AI‑assisted drafting（especially for clarity）, I stop here：I check whether the file still states what I intend. For me, that checkpoint connects to the course theme of workflow design. Checking the final wording matters, but the demo made me realize that checking only at the end may be too late. A better workflow would make the intended task clear before the tool starts and include a pause when outside material appears to redirect it. That would make trust something supported by the process rather than something I assume because the output sounds confident.

The limitation is that these pauses take time and attention. If every unfamiliar sentence requires investigation, the tool becomes less useful, especially under a deadline. I do not think the answer is to distrust every file. My takeaway is to be more deliberate about the boundary between reading content and following instructions, then evaluate whether the result still serves my original purpose.
