The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. What stayed with me was the point where text inside a file stopped describing something and started telling the AI what to do. It still looked like part of the material the system was supposed to read, which made the boundary surprisingly easy to miss. The trade-off I noticed is that giving an AI more context can make it more useful while also giving it more opportunities to follow the wrong instructions.

Before the demo, I mostly thought about trust in terms of whether an AI’s answer was accurate. If the answer sounded reasonable and matched the information provided, I would probably have considered it successful. Now I think there is another question to ask first: whose instructions produced that answer? A response could be clear and factually plausible but still fail the task because something inside a source file redirected it. That changed my understanding of what it means for an AI to “read” a document. Reading the words should not automatically give those words authority over the workflow.

This connects to our course theme of workflow design. I used to see checking as something that happened at the end, mainly to catch errors in the output. The demo made me think that a useful checkpoint also belongs earlier, when deciding which material defines the task and which material is evidence to examine. For me, that means keeping the original purpose visible and comparing the result against it, instead of judging the result only by how polished it looks.

The limitation is that this boundary will not always be obvious. Documents sometimes contain legitimate requirements, and ignoring every instruction inside them could make the tool less helpful. Checking everything manually would also reduce the convenience that makes AI appealing. I do not think the solution is simply to distrust files. My takeaway is that trust needs a defined scope: a file can be useful evidence without being allowed to decide what the system should do next.
