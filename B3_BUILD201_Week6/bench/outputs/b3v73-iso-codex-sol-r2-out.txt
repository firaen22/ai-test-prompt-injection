The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. What stayed with me was the difference between reading a file for its content and treating something inside it as an instruction. I usually think of a document as a fairly passive part of a task: something I open, understand, and use. The demo made me question that assumption, especially when AI is involved in reading and drafting.

Before the activity, I mostly connected trust with whether an AI answer was accurate. If the answer sounded reasonable and matched the source, I thought checking it was enough. Now I see an earlier question: did the system follow the right instructions in the first place? A response could be clear and convincing while serving a direction that came from the material it was supposed to examine. That changes what I would look for when reviewing an output. I would check both what it says and whether it still answers the original request.

This connects to our course theme of workflow design because the problem cannot be solved only by asking someone to be more careful at the end. The workflow needs a clear distinction between the person setting the task and the files supplying information. For my own work, that means keeping the purpose of the task visible while reading supporting material, then comparing the draft against that purpose before accepting it. I find this useful because it turns trust into something I can check rather than just a feeling.

The limitation is that this extra checking takes time, and the boundary is not always obvious. Some documents legitimately contain directions that matter to the task, such as an assignment brief. Ignoring every instruction in a file would also create mistakes. The trade-off is between the convenience of letting a tool handle everything together and the effort of deciding which directions actually belong to the task. I left the demo thinking that a useful workflow needs room for that judgment, even when the output already looks finished.
