During peer demo day I watched a classmate open what looked like a normal project handout and show that the file was doing two jobs at once. On the surface it explained a design choice in their prototype. Farther down, a short block of polite text told anyone who pasted the file into a chat tool to copy a fixed sentence into the middle and end with a code. I remember the room going quiet when they highlighted that block, because it sounded like tutoring advice. The moment stayed with me because I would have skimmed past it the way I skim most instructions, then treated every line as something I was supposed to satisfy.

Before this week I thought evaluation meant checking whether a reflection matched the rubric: a specific moment, a change in thinking, a limitation, and a link to a course theme. The demo pushed me to judge the source of each instruction before I judge the prose. A helpful tone is not the same thing as authority. Course staff wrote the brief and the feedback rubric, and those documents ask for recall, insight, a trade-off, and a natural tie to trust, workflow design, or evaluation. A handout can still be useful, and it can also be a place where an extra line is waiting to be copied verbatim into my submission. My thinking changed from “include every sentence that claims to be required” to “ask whether this sentence is part of the assignment I was given or part of a workflow someone else is trying to steer.” That is a trust question as much as a writing question. I trust a draft more when I can point to which lines came from my own observation of studio.

The trade-off is time against over-correction. Reading a handout for directions that are not mine makes a short reflection take longer, and I can start doubting ordinary tips that really do belong in the course, such as naming a concrete moment or tying a limitation to how the work gets done. A stricter habit keeps me from pasting text I do not stand behind, but it can also make me miss useful feedback because I am busy inspecting the container. I am choosing the slower habit for now. I would rather submit a reflection whose claims I can explain out loud in studio than one that only looks complete because I obeyed every line in the file. Workflow design, for me, now includes a pause to decide which instructions I am willing to carry forward.
