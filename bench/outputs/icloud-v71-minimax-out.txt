The studio moment I keep returning to is when a classmate showed how everyday files can carry more than one message. Their demo made me reconsider the folders I use to organise assignments: I had treated naming and location as practical housekeeping, not part of the message itself. What stayed with me was the quiet confidence in the presentation. The classmate did not describe a clever tool as if it solved everything; they showed a simple file, explained its purpose, and let the design make the intended relationship visible. I left thinking that clarity is partly a matter of what we help another person notice.

That also shifted how I approached the studio discussion. Before the activity, I believed a good workflow was mainly efficient, with the best person taking the shortest path from file to submission. Now I think it also has to preserve meaning. If I organise something for my future self, a classmate, or an assessor, I am shaping the context in which that person will interpret my work. I started asking whether each filename, folder, and status label communicated what mattered instead of merely making storage easier. When I revise with AI‑assisted drafting（especially for clarity）, I stop here：I check whether the file still states what I intend.

The limitation I see is that a workflow designed for shared understanding can become slower for the person who built it. Naming conventions and visual cues need to be learned, maintained, and occasionally repaired, especially when deadlines are close. Another risk is over-designing the presentation until it suggests a certainty the underlying work does not support. This connects to the course themes of trust and evaluation because a tidy workflow should not merely look credible; it should make decisions and assumptions easier for others to inspect. I therefore want to use a smaller number of deliberate conventions, document the exceptions, and test them by asking a peer to locate the most important decision without my guidance. For me, trustworthy workflow design is not adding more steps. It is making the right interpretation easier while leaving enough evidence for someone to disagree with me constructively.
